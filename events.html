Keynote · 9 July

Unified AI: Converging Frontier Capability, Security and Efficiency

Aaron examined how AI is reshaping the enterprise landscape — the ever-expanding attack surface, the real cost of tokens, and where enterprise AI adoption is heading next. Hear his argument in his own words:

AI security is a different game

In an interview with the Hong Kong Economic Times, Aaron argued that the security threats enterprises face when adopting AI are fundamentally different from traditional cybersecurity:

  • The barrier to attack has collapsed. Attackers no longer need deep technical knowledge — anyone who understands how AI works can exploit it, often just by using language or psychology to persuade a model.
  • AI agents open a new attack surface. As companies deploy agents to read documents, organise data and handle email, attackers can embed hidden instructions that only the AI can read. “There’s no need to break through a firewall — the AI will follow the instructions and execute whatever they want.”
  • Guardrails don’t hold for long. Model guardrails are broken quickly, and new vulnerabilities emerge faster than in conventional cybersecurity — making dedicated AI security essential.

The answer: Unified AI governance

Aaron’s view is that the future of AI governance lies in consolidating the enterprise “AI stack” into a single management platform — Unified AI — much as cloud computing once unified fragmented infrastructure. That gives organisations full visibility into which models and agents their people use, and whether they are used safely, systematising today’s ungoverned “AI sprawl” before it compounds risk across the business.
